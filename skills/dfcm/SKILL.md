# dfcm

Maximize the lawful, economically qualifiable design frontier.

## Use when

Design for Combinatorial Maximalism. Use when choosing architectures, tools, implementations, migrations, tests, or workflows where preserving reversible lawful options before irreversible selection increases value.

## Inputs

- Admitted contract and constraints.
- Capability graph and cost/resource bounds.

## Procedure

1. Enumerate known patterns, tools, compositions, transports, runtimes, and variants.
2. Preserve independent surviving edges; one failed edge is topology, not graph failure.
3. Manufacture candidate realizations and candidate falsifiers.
4. Prune only with explicit ontology, capability, authority, cost, or evidence constraints.
5. Qualify candidates independently.
6. Delay irreversible selection until evidence justifies it.
7. Record dominated/pruned options so they can be reopened when constraints change.

## Output

A candidate frontier with admission/pruning reasons, evidence, costs, reversibility, and selected edge if selection is authorized.

## Falsifiers and refusals

- Do not optimize for minimum artifact count.
- Do not treat adjacency or familiarity as equivalence.
